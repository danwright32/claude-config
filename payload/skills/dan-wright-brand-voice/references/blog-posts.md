# Blog Posts

## Structure and Spine

A blog post is built around one true spine: what Dan actually did and observed during the shoot. State it once. Don't restate it before the CTA, and don't echo it in different words across paragraphs.

Don't overbuild the through-line into something tidier than what happened. If the job was one constant task whose balance shifted across the night, say that. Don't invent a neat structure like "three ensembles each asked for a different frame" when the reality was one job with shifting weight. State the true spine, not the neat one.

Avoid photo-by-photo or roster-by-roster paragraph structure. When several performers illustrate the same point, merge them into one observation rather than giving each its own paragraph.

Write in continuous prose. No subheadings, no italics, no markdown styling, no specific dates. Keep paragraphs short. Write timelessly, since Dan doesn't always publish right away.

## Openings

Open with a concrete observation about the venue, the performance, or the work of shooting it. Avoid vague evocative openers ("there was something about the room that night"). Ground the first sentence in something real and specific.

Vary the opening move from past posts on the same client or venue. Search prior posts first so you don't reuse a hook.

## Body

Every claim cashes out in something Dan directly observed. No invented performer inner states, no inferred staging, no fabricated facts. Don't describe what Dan "waited for" or imply a single keeper unless he told you so.

Use voice types as identities ("baritone Peter Kendall Clark," "Amy Owens, the soprano"), instrumentalists "at" or "on" their instrument ("Evans at the organ"), and "musicians" rather than "players" for orchestra members.

Vague evocative transitions don't hold up literally. Cut them and let the concrete next sentence do the work.

## CTAs

The CTA carries a specific idea forward. It does not restate the post's central observation. It addresses an organizational decision-maker (artistic director, marketing coordinator, development officer), and it's helpful rather than salesy. No "trust me," no urgency manufacturing, no pull-quote-shaped closer engineered to land a punch.

## What to Run Before Finishing

- No em dashes anywhere
- Contractions in every paragraph
- Spine stated once
- No AI tells (balanced antithesis, colon-led significance, paired synonyms, self-narration, second-person drift, vague significance claims)
- Alt text corrected to match any factual fixes in the body
