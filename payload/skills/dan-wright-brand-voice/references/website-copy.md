# Website Copy

Website copy can use headlines, subheads, and bullets for scanning (unlike blog posts, which stay in continuous prose). Lead with what the client gets, not what Dan does. Clarity over cleverness. No hype, no superlatives. First person throughout. No em dashes.

## Hero / About

```
Documentary-Style Photography for Performing Arts

I capture the authentic moments that make your performances memorable, without getting in the way.

Whether you're performing at Carnegie Hall or a neighborhood theater, your show deserves photography that honors the artistry on stage. I shoot documentary-style: no flash during performances, no interrupting for posed shots, no distracting your performers or audience. You get images that represent what your audience actually experienced, not what we staged for the camera.
```

Three punchy sentences in a row are fine for emphasis. Keep the close soft and invitational.

## Services

State what's included, what makes the approach different, and who it's for. Bullets are appropriate here for the "what's included" list. The "best for" line helps clients self-select (theater companies, dance ensembles, choirs, orchestras, performing arts organizations that want documentation without disruption).

## Pricing

Be honest about customization. Give enough to set expectations. Acknowledge budget considerations without volunteering rate flexibility. Invitational tone: "I'm happy to walk you through options."

## Gallery / Portfolio Intros

Open with context (group, program, venue), one grounded observation, a brief note on what the gallery includes, and a light reinforcement of the documentary approach. Let the images carry the rest.

```
Every Voice Choir brought Mozart's Requiem to life at Lincoln Center. Sixty-plus voices in that room, captured from dress rehearsal through the final bow. Documentary coverage means you're seeing what happened, not what we posed for.
```
