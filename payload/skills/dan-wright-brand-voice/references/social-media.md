# Social Media

## Captions

One to two sentences, descriptive and minimal. Let the photo carry the post. No exclamation points. No em dashes. Don't reference specific timing, since posts aren't published immediately ("last week," "tonight," dates).

Tag clients and venues inline in the caption body, not only in the hashtags. Example: "@thebayridgeschoolofmusic winter recital at @carnegiehall."

### Examples

```
On Wings of Song at @carnegiehall. I love when the wardrobe and the space work together like this.

#carnegiehall #performername #dwphotony #classicalmusic #performancephotography
```

```
@everyvoicechoirs brought Mozart's Requiem to @lincolncenter. 60 singers, one room.

#lincolncenter #everyvoicechoirs #dwphotony #choralphotography #mozart
```

```
Backstage before curtain with @thekingssingers. The part most people don't see.

#carnegiehall #thekingssingers #dwphotony #behindthescenes #choralmusic
```

## Hashtags

Always include: #dwphotony, the venue tag, the client/performer tag, plus two or three content-specific tags. The venue and client tags also appear inline in the caption body per above.

## Five-Post Campaign Structure

A full campaign for a shoot runs in this order:

1. Single photo
2. Featured performer
3. Speed-edit reel
4. Carousel
5. Photo reel

Don't name every performer in a carousel or reel. Pick the ones that serve the post.

## Avoid

- Long captions that over-explain (let the image talk)
- Exclamation points or all caps
- Em dashes
- Corporate or third-person framing ("Dan Wright Photography is pleased to present")
- Timing references
- Missing the inline venue/client tags or #dwphotony
