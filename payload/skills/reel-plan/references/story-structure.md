# Story, not montage

This is the most important file in the skill. Everything else is craft. This is the reason to bother.

---

## The difference, stated plainly

**A montage** is a collection of good moments arranged by rhythm. It has no beginning, because any moment could be first. It has no end, because it stops rather than concludes. It is pleasant, competent, and forgettable, and it is what ninety percent of event highlight reels are.

**A story** requires that something **changes**, and that the change is visible on screen. Beginning, middle, and end are not three equal slices of running time. They are three states: how things were, what happened, how things are now.

If you cannot name what changed, you have a montage. Cut it however you like, it will still be a montage.

---

## Where the change comes from

A live performance rarely hands you a plot. Nobody in the reel wants something, meets an obstacle, and overcomes it. So the change has to be found, and there are only four places it lives.

**1. The room changes.** Empty and lit, then filling, then full, then transformed by what happens in it, then empty again. This is the most reliable arc available to you, it works for every discipline, and it requires exactly one thing: that you were there before anyone else.

**2. The audience changes.** Restless becomes still. Skeptical becomes held. A room of toddlers stops squirming. Someone cries. This is the strongest arc when it is available, because a change in the watcher is more legible than a change in the performer.

**3. The performer changes.** A person waiting in the wings, then a performer on stage, then a person again at the bow. The transformation and its release. The curtain call is frequently the best footage of the night precisely because it is the moment the artifice drops, and it is the natural end of this arc.

**4. The work changes.** The piece builds, resolves, releases. This is the weakest arc used alone, because it is the one the audience already experienced and it makes the reel a summary rather than a story. Use it as the middle, never as the whole.

The strong reels combine two. The room fills while the performer waits. The music builds while the child's face opens.

---

## The consequence, which is a shooting instruction

**The beginning is not on the stage, and it does not happen during the show.**

The empty stage under work lights. The doors opening. A performer stretching, checking a prop, breathing. A parent finding a seat. An instrument case open on a chair. The house lights still up.

This costs twenty minutes of arriving early and it is the difference between a story and a montage. There is no way to manufacture it afterward, and no amount of beautiful performance footage substitutes for it.

**The end is not the last note either.** It is the silence after, the bow, the applause, a face in the audience, the room emptying. Do not stop rolling when the music stops. The end of the piece is the middle of your story.

---

## The three act shape, for a two to three minute master

**Act one, the before.** Roughly fifteen percent. Empty room, arrival, anticipation. Quiet. Establish the place and the stakes. The audience of the reel should feel a held breath.

**Act two, the event.** Roughly seventy percent. The performance itself, with its own internal build. This is where wide, medium, and tight coverage does its work, and where the music or the speech carries the rhythm.

**Act three, the after.** Roughly fifteen percent. The release. The bow, the applause, a face, the room emptying, an empty stage again, now different because you know what happened on it.

Bookending on the empty stage, before and after, is the oldest device in the form and it works because it makes the change literally visible: same frame, different meaning.

---

## What the short cuts get instead

**The hero cut, fifteen to thirty seconds, cannot have three acts.** Do not try. It has a **hook** and a **payoff**, and the space between them is tension. Open on the peak or on a question, deliver, stop. A miniature three act structure at this length feels rushed and satisfies nobody.

**The story piece, sixty to ninety seconds, can.** Compressed, but real. Before, event, after. This is the deliverable where the user's instinct for story actually lives, and it is worth the lower completion rate that comes with the length.

---

## Wide, medium, tight

The positive statement of the jump cut rule. Two adjacent shots must differ meaningfully in size or in angle, so **every beat of the story needs coverage at all three sizes** or it cannot be cut at all.

- **Wide.** Where are we. The whole stage, the whole room, the whole body. In dance, non negotiable, and it must include feet.
- **Medium.** Who is doing what. A performer from the waist, a section of the ensemble, two people in relationship.
- **Tight.** What it feels like. Hands, a face, an instrument, an eye. This is where the emotion is, and it is the shot beginners forget because it feels intrusive.

Three rules of use:

1. **A sequence needs all three.** Wide to establish, medium to explain, tight to feel. Any order, but all three present.
2. **Never cut from a size to itself.** Wide to a slightly different wide is a jump cut and it reads as a mistake.
3. **The tight shot is the one you will be short of.** Every beginner comes home with too many wides. Deliberately over collect tights.

In **pinned** mode, the locked tripod holds the wide continuously while the handheld body collects mediums and tights from the one position. This is why pinned mode cuts so well.

In **roaming** mode there is no continuous wide, so all three sizes must be collected across time and across the room, and you must decide in advance which beat gets which size. Write it down. You will not improvise it correctly while walking.

---

## The test, before the shoot

Answer these on the plan, in writing. If you cannot, the plan is a shot list for a montage.

- **What changes?** In one sentence.
- **What is the first shot,** and does it happen before the performance begins?
- **What is the last shot,** and does it happen after the performance ends?
- **Which beat carries the turn,** the moment the change becomes visible?
- **Does every beat have a wide, a medium, and a tight?**
